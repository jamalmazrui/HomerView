# HomerView

For thirty years, blind people have read the web through a flat copy of the
page that a screen reader builds. It works, and it is how most of us read
everything. But the browser knows a great deal that never reaches that copy.
Every blind reader has met the gap: a button you are told is not there, a
banner you never knew was covering the screen, a dialog that swallows your
keystrokes without saying so.

HomerView is a free NVDA add-on that closes the gap. It runs its own copy of
Microsoft Edge and talks to it directly, the way a web developer's tools do.
So the page is there three ways at once: as a window, as your reading order,
and as the browser itself understands it. Almost nothing else has all three,
and everything HomerView does comes from having them together. Press Y and it
describes what a sighted person can see and your reading order never mentions.
Press Alt+NVDA+A and it tests the page for accessibility problems, finds the
publisher's contact address, and writes the report as an email. Press Control+O
and it opens a Word file, a slide deck, a PDF or an ebook as a web page, so all
eighty of its commands work on it.

No artificial intelligence is involved, and nothing about the pages you read
leaves your computer. Everything you need is free or already installed: NVDA is
a free download, and Microsoft Edge comes with Windows. HomerView is free
software under the same licence as NVDA itself.

You can [download the installer from the latest
release](https://github.com/JamalMazrui/HomerView/releases/latest), or [read
the source on the HomerView project
page](https://github.com/JamalMazrui/HomerView). If you do not have NVDA yet,
it is a [free download from NV Access](https://www.nvaccess.org/download).
