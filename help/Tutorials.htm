0 - Overview

What HomerView is, two keys of the screen reader’s own, and the table of contents.

Before you start: Nothing is needed; this walk is listened to.

Step 1

HomerView opens its own copy of Microsoft Edge and talks to the browser directly, so it can answer what a screen reader cannot: the page as its scripts built it, what is at the other end of a link before you go, and files behind a sign-in fetched as a click would fetch them. It adds a command only where your screen reader cannot do the job, or cannot do it as well.

Step 2: Insert+Up Arrow

Two keys before anything else, both the reader’s own. If a line goes by too fast, Insert plus Up Arrow says it again.

Screen reader:

  • (the last line, read a second time)

Step 3: Insert+Tab

And if you lose your place, Insert plus Tab says where you are.

Screen reader:

  • (the current control, with its state and position)

Step 4

Now the table of contents. I say the number and the title; the reader says what the walk covers.

Step 5

One, User Interface.

Screen reader:

  • The HomerView window, the menu of every command, the hotkey summary, the rules its keys follow, and where help lives.

Step 6

Two, Install and Launch.

Screen reader:

  • The installer, the scripts it sets up for your screen reader, and the key that opens HomerView.

Step 7

Three, Read an Article Properly.

Screen reader:

  • Past the navigation in one key, the article alone in a tab, and the whole page read without moving.

Step 8

Four, Decide Whether to Follow a Link.

Screen reader:

  • What is at the other end of a link, before you go there.

Step 9

Five, Find Things.

Screen reader:

  • Searching backwards, searching by pattern, and gathering every match.

Step 10

Six, Select and Copy a Passage.

Screen reader:

  • A passage selected in two keys, copied, and passages gathered from across a page.

Step 11

Seven, Gather Files from a Page.

Screen reader:

  • Every document on a page fetched at once, with the browser’s own sign-in.

Step 12

Eight, Check Accessibility.

Screen reader:

  • Two accessibility engines over a page, readable reports, and who to tell.

Step 13

Nine, Conclusion.

Screen reader:

  • What to carry away, the words HomerView uses, the help built in, and where to learn more.

Step 14

Ten walks, each under five minutes, under an hour together: the overview, the user interface, seven tasks, and the conclusion. They are a course, not a reference: each one assumes those before it.

Something to try: Take walks one and two, then the task you need today.

1 - User Interface

HomerView’s window and how it is reached, the menu of every command, the hotkey summary, the rule that it adds only what the screen reader cannot do, the patterns its keys follow, and where help lives.

Before you start: HomerView is installed.

Step 1

HomerView is a browser window of its own, with your screen reader in charge of reading it. I name each part; the reader shows it.

Step 2: Alt+Control+Shift+H

Alt plus Control plus Shift plus H opens the HomerView browser, or brings it back.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 3: Insert+T

Its own window, on a start page, in a separate browser profile, so your ordinary browsing is untouched. The same key later brings you back rather than opening another. Every other HomerView key works only while this window is in front. Insert plus T, the reader’s own key, says the title.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 4: Alt+F10

Nothing is hidden behind a key you must already know. Alt plus F10 opens a list of every command, each with its key beside it.

Screen reader:

  • Commands list box

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 5: Down Arrow

Down Arrow moves through them; a command’s name, then its key.

Screen reader:

  • (the next command and its key)

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 6: Alt+Shift+H

Escape closes the list. Alt plus Shift plus H is the hotkey summary: every command as a line you can press Enter on to run it. Its first line is the version and when it was installed.

Screen reader:

  • (the version, and when it was installed)

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 7: Escape

Escape. Now the rule behind HomerView: it adds a command only where your screen reader cannot do the job, or does it noticeably worse. So it has no list of headings or links: your reader has those. It searches backwards, and by pattern, because readers do not.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 8: Alt+L

The key patterns are the kit’s. The letter is the first letter of a word: Alt plus L, L for Link. Shift reverses: Control plus F3 searches forward by pattern, Control plus Shift plus F3 backwards. F8 is selection: F8 starts it, Shift plus F8 completes it.

Screen reader:

  • (what is at the other end of the link)

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 9: Shift+Q

A few keys depend on the screen reader, because each reserves different letters: Jump to Probable Main is Shift plus Q, or, with some screen readers, Shift plus J. The menu shows the right one for yours.

Screen reader:

  • Main content, as the page declares it

Step 10

Help is in four places. I say which; the reader says the key. Every command, by name.

Screen reader:

  • Alt plus F10, the command list

Step 11

Every key, with the version.

Screen reader:

  • Alt plus Shift plus H, the hotkey summary

Step 12

The guide and the hotkeys, as documents.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView dot m d, and hotkeys dot m d

Step 13: Alt+Control+Shift+H

A planned misstep: pressing a HomerView key in another program does nothing for HomerView; that program gets the key. Alt plus Control plus Shift plus H brings HomerView back first.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 14

What this walk taught. I name the command; the reader gives the key.

Step 15

Every command, by name.

Screen reader:

  • Alt plus F10

Step 16

Every key, with the version.

Screen reader:

  • Alt plus Shift plus H

Something to try: Open the menu of every command and find three commands you did not know.

2 - Install and Launch

The installer finds your screen reader and sets up its scripts; then the key that opens HomerView from anywhere.

Before you start: HomerView_setup.exe is downloaded from the HomerView page on GitHub.

Step 1

One want: HomerView working with your screen reader, without a restart.

Step 2: Enter

I open the downloaded setup program and allow it to make changes.

Screen reader:

  • Setup - HomerView dialog
  • Welcome to the HomerView Setup Wizard

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 3: Enter

Enter takes Next on each page; the defaults are right.

Screen reader:

  • Select Destination Location

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 4: Tab

The last page offers the scripts for each screen reader it finds on this computer, ticked, each saying Install, Update or Reinstall. For one reader it also compiles the scripts for every version installed and asks the reader to reload them, so no restart is normally needed.

Screen reader:

  • (the box for your screen reader’s scripts, its first word, checked)

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 5: Space

A planned misstep: clearing that box. HomerView’s keys would then do nothing. Space ticks it back.

Screen reader:

  • checked

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 6: Enter

Enter installs.

Screen reader:

  • Finish button

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 7: Alt+Control+Shift+H

The key on HomerView’s desktop icon opens it from anywhere, with any screen reader or none.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 8: Alt+Shift+H

To confirm which build is loaded: the hotkey summary’s first line.

Screen reader:

  • (the version, and when it was installed)

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 9

If the opening key does nothing, another program has taken it; set a different one in the icon’s properties. What this walk taught. I name the step; the reader gives the key.

Step 10

Open HomerView.

Screen reader:

  • Alt plus Control plus Shift plus H

Step 11: Control+F1

The documentation is a key away from any page. Control plus F1 opens the user guide in the HomerView window; plain F1 stays Edge’s own help.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView User Guide

Step 12: Alt+Shift+F1

Alt plus Shift plus F1 opens the quick start, the first ten minutes of HomerView, for anyone new to it.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView Quick Start

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 13: Alt+F1

Shift plus F1 opens the history of changes, and Alt plus F1 says the version and where HomerView keeps its files.

Screen reader:

  • About HomerView

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 14: Alt+Shift+H

Alt plus Shift plus H, Hotkey Summary, lists every command with its key in a window of its own, the quickest way to learn the keys.

Screen reader:

  • Hotkey Summary

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 15: Alt+F10

Alt plus F10, the Alternate Menu, lists every command in one alphabetized list, for the moment a key will not come to mind.

Screen reader:

  • Alternate Menu

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 16: Escape

Escape closes it without running anything.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView

Step 17

Control plus F11 checks for a newer HomerView and installs it, as F11 does in other Homer programs.

HomerView keeps its own copy of the browser, so its settings and its sign ins never disturb your everyday Edge.

Step 20: Alt+Shift+B

Alt plus Shift plus B, Choose Browser, picks which Chromium browser it drives, from those installed here.

Screen reader:

  • Choose Browser dialog

Step 21: Escape

Escape leaves the choice as it was.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView

Which build is loaded.

3 - Read an Article Properly

Past the navigation in one key, the article alone in a tab of its own, and the whole page read from the top without moving the cursor.

Before you start: HomerView is open on a news article with a lot of navigation around it.

Step 1

One want: the article, not the menus, links and footers around it.

Step 2: Alt+Control+Shift+H

Alt plus Control plus Shift plus H opens the HomerView browser, or brings it back.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 3: Shift+Q

Shift plus Q is Jump to Probable Main; where your screen reader keeps Q for itself, HomerView puts it on Shift plus J, and Hotkey Summary, Alt plus Shift plus H, shows which. On a page that declares its main region, I land there.

Screen reader:

  • Main content, as the page declares it

Step 4: Shift+Q

On a page that declares nothing, HomerView weighs every part – how much text, how much of it links – and moves to the winner.

Screen reader:

  • Main content, by weighing the page

Step 5: Shift+F9

Shift plus F9 is Extract Main Content: the article, its title, byline and links, saved and opened in a tab of its own, without the surrounding furniture.

Screen reader:

  • (the article’s title, as a heading)

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 6: Alt+F8

Every HomerView command works on that tab, because it is a real page. Alt plus F8 is Read All: it reads the whole page from the top, and does not move the cursor.

Screen reader:

  • (the page, from the top)

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 7

A planned misstep: the screen reader’s own Say All starts where you are and leaves you further down. Read All starts at the top and leaves you where you were.

Step 8

What this walk taught. I name the command; the reader gives the key.

Step 9

Jump past the navigation.

Screen reader:

  • Shift plus Q

Step 10

The article alone.

Screen reader:

  • Shift plus F9

Step 11: Control+Down Arrow

Inside the article, Control plus Down Arrow moves by paragraph and reads it, as it does in Word.

Screen reader:

  • The study followed four hundred families for ten years.

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 12: Alt+Down Arrow

Alt plus Down Arrow moves by sentence, for a closer reading.

Screen reader:

  • Most moved at least once.

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 13: Alt+Up Arrow

Alt plus Up Arrow goes back a sentence to hear it again.

Screen reader:

  • The study followed four hundred families for ten years.

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 14: Z

Z moves to the next thing of the same kind as the one I am on: the next paragraph, the next list item, the next link.

Screen reader:

  • The results surprised the authors.

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 15: Alt+Z

Alt plus Z moves to the next thing of a different kind, past a long run of links to the text after them.

Screen reader:

  • heading level 2, Methods

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 16: Shift+F9

Shift plus F9 sets the readable part of the page apart, in a page of its own, without the menus and advertisements around it.

Screen reader:

  • Extracted main content

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 17: Alt+Delete

Alt plus Delete says where I am: the line, the column and how far through the page.

Screen reader:

  • Line 42, column 1, 38 percent

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 18: ScrollLock

Scroll Lock starts reading continuously, and stops it again, when I would rather listen than step.

Screen reader:

  • Reading

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 19: Alt+M

Alt plus M says what the page claims about itself: its author, its publisher and its date.

Screen reader:

  • Author: Lena Ortiz. Published 3 March 2026

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 20

Read all, without moving.

Screen reader:

  • Alt plus F8

Something to try: Open a long article, jump to its main content, and extract it.

4 - Decide Whether to Follow a Link

Alt plus L asks a link’s server what is at the other end – a page, a PDF, an archive – how big, its title, about how long to read, any paywall, and where it really goes.

Before you start: HomerView is open on a page with links.

Step 1

One want: to know what a link leads to before spending the time to follow it.

Step 2: Alt+Control+Shift+H

Alt plus Control plus Shift plus H opens the HomerView browser, or brings it back.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 3: Alt+L

I move to a link. Alt plus L, L for Link, is Link Target.

Screen reader:

  • (a web page, its title, about how many words and minutes)

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 4

HomerView asked the link’s server without fetching the whole thing: what kind of thing it is, how big, its title if it is a page, roughly how long to read, and whether there is a paywall.

Step 5: Alt+L

I move to a shortened link and ask again.

Screen reader:

  • (where the short address really goes)

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 6

Shortened links are resolved and named, and a link that ends somewhere other than where it seemed to point says so.

Step 7: Alt+L

A planned misstep: a link to a server that cannot be reached. HomerView still says its address, which was known before anything was asked.

Screen reader:

  • (the address, and that it could not be reached)

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 8

What this walk taught. I name the command; the reader gives the key.

Step 9: Alt+U

Alt plus U says where the link at the cursor would go, its address, before I decide anything.

Screen reader:

  • https colon slash slash example dot org slash report dot pdf

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 10

Link Target, Alt plus L, goes further: what kind of thing is there, how big it is, and whether it ends somewhere else.

Step 11: Alt+L

For a document, it says the kind and the size, so a large download is no surprise.

Screen reader:

  • PDF document, 4.2 megabytes

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 12: Alt+L

For a link that redirects, it says where it really lands, which is how a disguised link shows itself.

Screen reader:

  • Redirects to a different site: example dot net

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 13: Alt+Shift+P

Alt plus Shift plus P copies every link address on the page to the clipboard, for checking a page’s links all at once.

Screen reader:

  • 24 links copied

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 14: Alt+Apostrophe

Alt plus Apostrophe says what is on the clipboard, to hear what was copied.

Screen reader:

  • https colon slash slash example dot org

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 15: Alt+A

Alt plus A says this page’s own address; pressed twice, it spells it; three times, it copies it.

Screen reader:

  • https colon slash slash example dot org slash news

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 16

Deciding first saves a trip to a page that was never wanted, and a download that was never meant.

Step 17: Alt+R

Alt plus R opens a recent page, for the moment a link already followed is wanted again.

Screen reader:

  • Recent Pages dialog

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 18

A link that opens a document rather than a page is common in government and university sites; asking first is how to know which it is.

Step 19: Alt+Q

Alt plus Q looks something up with free services that need no account, such as a word in the link’s text that is unfamiliar.

Screen reader:

  • Query Web dialog

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 20: Escape

Escape closes it, and the cursor is back on the link.

Screen reader:

  • link, Annual report

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 21

What is at the other end of a link.

Screen reader:

  • Alt plus L

Something to try: Ask about three links before following any of them.

5 - Find Things

Searching backwards, which a screen reader’s own find does not, searching by pattern, moving between matches, and gathering every match into one window.

Before you start: HomerView is open on a page with dates or prices on it.

Step 1

One want: the years mentioned on a long page, and the place where one appears. Your reader’s own Control plus F searches forward, and HomerView leaves it alone.

Step 2: Alt+Control+Shift+H

Alt plus Control plus Shift plus H opens the HomerView browser, or brings it back.

Screen reader:

  • HomerView

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 3: Control+Shift+F

Control plus Shift plus F searches backwards, which the screen reader’s find cannot.

Screen reader:

  • Find Backwards dialog

Confirm the wording with a live run: buildTutorials -live.

Step 4: Control+F3
